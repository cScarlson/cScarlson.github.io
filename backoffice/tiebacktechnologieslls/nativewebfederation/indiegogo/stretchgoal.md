## The Precedent: Why We Are Funding This Independently
Turning directly to the developer community to finance open digital public infrastructure is an established and highly successful framework. Recent history proves that the ecosystem is deeply hungry for native browser standards and is entirely willing to fund the teams building them.

- **The Baseline Validation**: When the creators of **Web Awesome** launched their initiative to bring a library of native browser Web Components to light, they set their exact foundational baseline funding target at **$30,000** — matching the baseline engineering safety net we have established for our launch. The global development community responded by hyper-funding their campaign to nearly **$800,000**.
- **The Scope Expansion**: While highly successful precedents like Web Awesome focused their engineering scope on delivering a standalone native layout asset library and Open SDK, our roadmap goes substantially further. The open WebSDK we are developing is merely a single onramp layer inside a much larger native paradigm. Stacking our foundational RMD pattern alongside our upcoming grant pipelines allows us to deliver the Open WebSDK plus cross-domain website federation, native user data sovereignty, the Content Domain Name Server (CDNS) infrastructure, and the final establishing framework of the independent Native Web Federation standards body.
- **The Infrastructure Precedent**: Technical infrastructure blocks like the **Keystone Framework** have also thoroughly demonstrated that rewards-based crowdfunding is the optimal vehicle to bridge early R&D gaps, allowing independent software teams to successfully package raw architectural spikes into universally compliant open standards.

Reaching this milestone completely transforms our engineering timeline, bypassing institutional grant review delays and moving us straight into an aggressive, direct-to-production development track. This capital completely secures our entire three-person engineering team’s full operational runway, keeping our core human infrastructure stably focused on the code.

Because this goal is manually unlocked based on our baseline stability, every threshold reached past our initial safety net dictates exactly how many architectural layers we can deliver directly to the digital commons via pure public demand. Securing this runway accelerates our transition past the baseline Phase 1 Core Specification, funding continuous development sprints to build out Phase 2 (Native Microfrontend Protocol), Phase 3 (Native Data Sovereignty), and our upcoming Open WebSDK frameworks. We will extract our validated alpha playground logic, deploy automated cross-domain QA conformance test runners, and establish the independent Native Web Federation standards body — completely self-sustained by the developers who will use it.

The market data is clear: developers want a permanent exit track from the corporate framework version treadmill. By backing our baseline safety net or helping us lock in our full operational stretch goals, you are joining an engineered, high-velocity movement to fund the next generation of global web standards directly from pure public demand.
