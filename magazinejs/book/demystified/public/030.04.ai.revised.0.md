### Before You Burn This Book

Please note: we are not saying all AI is inherently evil, nor that machine learning has no place in civilization. When we use the shorthand "AI" in this chapter, we are talking specifically about the unmitigated garbage wave brought on by the **Broad-Use LLM industry**.

In reality, machine learning and narrow AI can be extraordinarily useful tools — namely in deterministic, strictly bounded applications. But when AI is applied as a universal hammer, users quickly slam face-first into a brutal **Law of Diminishing Returns**.

For an experienced developer, relying on a broad-use LLM is often a net-negative for productivity. By the time you have narrowed the model’s scope down enough through tireless prompt engineering, token-wrangling, and fixing subtle hallucinations, you could have written the solution yourself twice over.

For junior developers, the trap is even more insidious. While LLMs can help junior engineers think through syntax errors or act as an interactive rubber duck, that "help" too often mirrors writing a Jira ticket for them. Instead of building mental models, the junior simply delegates their cognitive processing to a black box. They bypass the struggle required to build architectural intuition, stunting their career growth before it even begins.

This chapter makes no apologies for being a one-sided critique. The pro-AI narrative is ubiquitous, blared at us from every keynote and earnings call. Here, we provide the counter-weight that is largely absent from the mainstream discourse.

### What I Am *Not* Worried About

A primary anxiety haunting the tech workforce is that AI will permanently eliminate human labor en masse. While we have certainly witnessed immense pain and structural damage from corporate "AI layoffs," economic history suggests this is a self-correcting — albeit brutal — loop.

Consider the feedback loop: **If a novice never gets hired, they never gain the foundational experience required to become a senior expert.** If the entry-level talent pipeline dries up, the industry stops producing the senior architects whose deep domain knowledge fuels the training data and oversight for future systems. Without human experts, AI models starve of novel insights, degrading into inbred, hallucination-prone parrots eating their own digital tails.

**No juniors, no seniors, no ROI.** The panic over total human obsolescence ignores the fundamental reality that AI is downstream of human expertise. Contrarywise, this is not what we hear from most mainstream narratives we are given.

### What I *Am* Worried About

The real dangers arrive the moment society takes broad-use LLMs at face value. These risks span a terrifying spectrum: from automated petty crime and hyper-targeted white-collar fraud to systemic economic shocks born from architectural limitations of LLMs, themselves.

While I am similarly unconcerned with Hollywood scenarios of AI "destroying the world" — though plausible vectors exist where deepfake social engineering turns human actors into unwitting proxies to bridge air-gapped or sensitive systems — I am far more terrified of AI simply *ruining* the world. The ultimate threat is not a dramatic global extinction event, but a slow descent into a drab, colorless, and deeply uninspiring existence. Beyond economic dislocation, an environment saturated with synthetic, machine-generated noise strips human culture of genuine texture and charm. The true hazard lies in creating an existential landscape that breeds widespread alienation, anxiety, and depression — deteriorating collective mental health to the point where elevated suicide rates and systemic despair become the baseline. Is a sterile, joyless world where humans are reduced to passive spectators really the society we are striving to engineer?

#### The Nigerian Prince Is King

If foundation models remain free or artificially subsidized by venture capital, we face an asymmetric security crisis. Every petty criminal on the street corner stealing a bikewheel now carries a smartphone equipped with generative capabilities.

The classic "Nigerian Prince" scam used to be easily filtered out by native human intuition, largely flagged by glaring grammatical errors and crude syntax. Today, generative models have automated professional-grade copywriting, voice cloning, and multilingual localization. The Nigerian Prince is now a king because his scams are indistinguishable from legitimate institutional communications.

This isn’t limited to phishing; it scales across the entire threat landscape. Bad actors are combining automated cyber-penetration tools with LLM-driven social engineering to map enterprise networks and manipulate employees simultaneously.

How can public welfare systems, municipal portals, and critical digital infrastructure survive an environment where every script-kiddie can launch hyper-personalized, AI-orchestrated Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) and social engineering campaigns at zero marginal cost? Global public sector surveys indicate that the vast majority of government agencies face mounting exposure to AI-empowered fraud, waste, and abuse schemes.

A more dystopian irony is when **we begin to deploy AI to fight AI**. It locks society into an escalating cyber-arms race — a perpetual cat-and-mouse game where human operators surrender operational control to autonomous algorithms simply to stay afloat.

##### One Solution: Federally Regulated Equipment

At some point, frontier AI models — particularly as they approach autonomous general capabilities — will have to transition into **federally regulated equipment**, akin to hazardous materials, dual-use chemicals, and cryptographic hardware. Society already restricts domains like:

* Explosives and Pyrotechnics (e.g., restricted energetic materials)
* Regulated Firearms and Tactical Weaponry
* Medical and Radiological Devices
* High-Power Telecommunications and Encrypted Radio Equipment

We have already seen hobbyists bypass safety guardrails to coax frontier models into providing step-by-step syntheses of dangerous chemical precursors and explosive devices. If citizens are legally barred from possessing amateur quantities of thermite without a federal license, why should a software tool that guides an untrained user through its creation remain completely unregulated?

Ultimately, advanced AI will likely be restricted to vetted research institutions, licensed enterprise engineers, and certified entities. If you aren't an enterprise employee shielded by corporate compliance budgets, I'd suggest getting through that backlog of "vibe-coding" projects while you still can. Restricting deployment to trusted entities might be the only way to avert the Nigerian King scenario — while conveniently transforming Machine Learning engineers into high-priced, white-glove compliance assets.

#### Scalability & Sustainability

Setting aside the environmental debate over carbon footprints, it is no secret that foundation model datacenters are thermodynamic monsters. They guzzle staggering volumes of municipal water for cooling and strain electrical grids to their breaking points.

The tragedy is not just the resource consumption; it’s *what* those resources are being burned for. Companies aren't unlocking profound scientific paradigm shifts — they are burning gigawatts of power to squeeze out marginal gains in code autocomplete and email drafting.

The financial balance sheets are staggering. As industry analysts and economic studies have highlighted, major players routinely burn billions in capital expenditure for incremental productivity bumps. Microsoft’s historic capital outlay on Copilot infrastructure reflects an unprecedented gap between upfront CapEx and actual software monetization.

**AI does not scale like traditional software.** Normal software exhibits a near-zero marginal cost of reproduction and a loose relationship between physical resources and business output. If a traditional software company wants to scale productivity, it hires senior engineers who build robust abstractions, streamline codebases, and drive compounding architectural leverage.

AI doesn't work that way. For every minute increase in output, an AI operation must throw colossal physical resources at the problem: real estate, custom silicon clusters, dedicated substations, and millions of gallons of cooling water. **AI scales like a hospital system or a heavy manufacturing plant, not an IT department.** And worse: the underlying infrastructure is entirely owned and controlled by a handful of tech monoliths, leaving you with zero operational leverage.

This reality is reflected in market data: **over half of enterprises that enacted reactionary "AI layoffs" have publicly reported regret**, quietly scrambling to rehire human personnel. That is the ultimate proof that current-generation AI falls far short of the utopian claims force-fed to the public.

##### The Anatomy of an LLM: The Memory Capacitor

To understand *why* AI hits these walls, you have to look under the hood. At its core, an LLM relies on a finite memory structure called the **Context Window**.

The mechanism is straightforward: you submit a prompt, it enters the context window, and if there is enough capacity, the model maps relational weights to generate a response. If your prompt exceeds capacity, older context is evicted or compressed to make room.

This hardware-level bottleneck exposes a profound disconnect between Silicon Valley hype and physical reality. Venture capitalists have been fed the narrative that current scaling failures are "just growing pains on the road to AGI".

It echoes the classic refrain of centralized economic planning: *"Just endure this temporary famine; the socialist utopia is right around the corner."*

If that promise proves false — as architectural ceilings suggest it will — then our fears about job displacement morph into something stranger. If AGI *is* achieved, our worst socio-economic fears materialize instantly. Yet, true machine novelty remains deeply theoretical. Evolution took millions of years, biological complexity, and planetary-scale trial-and-error to forge the human brain. To replicate that level of autonomous agency requires more than brute-force matrix algebra; it requires thermodynamic and evolutionary pressures we cannot simply synthesize in a server rack.

###### Achieving a Broader Context Window
If you believe that simply broadening the context window is the final frontier holding us back from AGI or Superintelligence, the empirical reality presents an ironic plot twist: expanding a model's window historically degrades its overall reasoning performance. In practice, as the context window scales, the model's output quality often degrades — a phenomenon researchers observe when critical information becomes "lost in the middle" of vast token sequences. My theory for this hardware-level failure is simple: much like the human mind, an intelligent system cannot process a infinite wall of data without an overarching framework to dictate what is meaningful out of the overwhelming noise. As psychologist Jordan Peterson reminds us, prioritizing relevance out of infinite complexity is the core practical function of a Value System. But how does a statistical neural net forge a genuine value system in the absolute absence of living, embodied experience? Even bound within a robotic chassis, synthetic code lacks the primal imperatives of life, mortality, and existential risk — the very biological motivations required to anchor a value hierarchy that reliably serves human ends.

For a deeper dive into how human perception relies on a value hierarchy to filter meaning out of noise, watch Where Do Our Values Come From? | Oz Talk With Jordan Peterson. Dr. Peterson breaks down how perception itself is impossible without an underlying structure of value to prioritize information.

#### The Entry-Level Brain vs. The Context Window

How does an LLM's static context window compete with the dynamic, expanding context of a human software engineer over a multi-year career?

Software engineering is intellectually grueling not because typing code is hard, but because of **scale**. What separates a senior architect from a novice is not typing speed — it is *how many layers of abstraction the developer can simultaneously hold in working memory* while anchoring their choices to a coherent system design.

Software architecture operates across distinct nested layers:

1. **Syntax:** Individual expressions rolling up into methods and functions.
2. **Encapsulation:** Methods and types organizing into classes and modules.
3. **Modular:** How distinct modules interface within a broader software subsystem.
4. **Architectural:** How application boundaries interact across distributed systems.
5. **Organizational:** How technical architectures mirror company teams and communication lines (Conway's Law).
6. **Business:** How sub-components align with macroeconomic realities and business models.

AI models struggle chronically to maintain consistency past the first two layers. When an LLM injects code into a complex repository, it operates via statistical local optimization. It finds a clever local patch to fix your immediate bug, but in doing so, it violates systemic constraints across layers 3 through 6.

This leads directly to **Architectural Skew**. Decades of AI research celebrate how models find "clever solutions humans wouldn't think of." In practice, this means the model rewrites your environment's baseline assumptions to solve a minor local symptom.

If you are curing cancer, radical biochemical mutation and algorithmic skew is welcome. If you are maintaining a deterministic software architecture, it is a nightmare. Before you know it, you are wrestling with a juggernaut of cascading integration bugs because the AI decided to alter your core layout to accommodate one extra HTML tag instead of using a standard CSS one-liner that a human engineer would instantly elect for.

We keep hearing that AI turns Junior Developers into Senior Developers but it looks more like the opposite — AI turns Seniors into Juniors:

* **Erosion of mental models:** Superficial familiarity with generated code blocks replaces deep comprehension of runtime execution flows and memory boundaries.
* **Atrophied debugging instincts:** A systemic inability to diagnose root causes or trace complex faults without pasting stack traces back into an LLM.
* **Compounding technical debt:** Blind acceptance of locally optimized snippets that silently violate global modular invariants and long-term maintainability.
* **Loss of architectural foresight:** Inability to project how a software codebase will scale or survive multi-year organizational lifecycles.
* **Lower confidence and persistent impostor syndrome:** Growing internal friction from realizing one is merely steering a black box rather than mastering the underlying engineering craft.
* **Low-to-no code reuse (high entropy):** Proliferation of redundant, prompt-dependent generated routines rather than clean, composable software abstractions.

### The UBI Illusion: Feudalism by Digital Allowance

When tech executives acknowledge the inevitable labor disruption, their silver bullet is Universal Basic Income (UBI). It is painted as a techno-humanitarian safety net where automation produces infinite abundance, and tech conglomerates distribute monthly stipends so citizens can survive.

In reality, UBI under AI hyper-consolidation is mathematically flawed and economically untenable. As research highlighted by Josh Risser in *Capitalism is in Crisis* demonstrates, market capitalism relies on an exchange of real productivity for consumer purchasing power. If AI displaces workers faster than new industries can generate genuine economic value, the tax base collapses. Funding a meaningful baseline wage for hundreds of millions of dislocated workers requires continuous wealth extraction from the very AI monopolies burning capital on datacenter infrastructure.

Worse, as analyst Brendan Dell exposes in his examinations of AI market crashes, the economic math behind tech sector bailouts and corporate-funded wealth redistribution shifts the burden onto the public while centralizing monetary power. If broad-use AI giants end up controlling global compute, proprietary algorithms, and automated productivity, granting a "free" digital allowance to a subjugated public transforms citizens from active economic participants into permanent corporate dependents.

This allowance will not arrive in sovereign currency; it will likely be distributed via proprietary, programmable central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) or corporate-backed crypto tokens. When a cluster of tech conglomerates owns the entire physical cloud, the compute infrastructure, and the transaction rails, they effectively supersede sovereign governments. You become entirely beholden to digital overlords who possess the direct authority to turn off your financial valve at the push of a button.

This dynamic echoes the famous warning often attributed to the early banking dynasty:

> *"Give me control over a nation's money, and I care not who writes its laws."*

### I'm With Michael Burry

When you look at the speculative mania, circular financing loops, and inflated valuations in the AI and semiconductor sectors, the parallels to past financial bubbles are impossible to ignore.

Before long, capital markets will demand a return on investment. As infrastructure costs mount, tool providers are forced to hike subscription prices. Eventually, the consumer market will saturate, pushing AI vendors toward B2B physical appliances — humanoid robotics or enterprise hardware leases.

Whether this speculative bubble bursts with a bang or deflates slowly, prominent macroeconomic bears like Michael Burry have placed massive short positions against the semiconductor and AI infrastructure complex.

History shows that market mania rarely stays isolated. When an employment crisis in knowledge work collides with over-leveraged asset bubbles, the cascading shockwaves will be felt across the entire economy.

In the case where AI giants stake their ROI on the public's ability to buy their products — the same products that put them out of work — and those people end up defaulting on their housing assets, this is where two bubbles collide. But that doesn't even have to happen for the AI bubble to take down the housing market; companies are already overleveraged on their commercial real-estate enough to make that a strong potential in the first place.

### Protecting Oneself: The Power of "Tieback"

In short, be fiercely strategic and selective in how you apply AI to your professional workflow. Do not outsource the cognitive scaffolding that makes you an expert.

As technology professionals, we must exercise our critical faculties to distinguish between genuine technological progress and hollow hype — a principle I call **"Tieback."** That is, *Tieback* is a technology's reflection of real-word application; you can have Positive Tieback or Negative. Technology that makes your life worse, instead of better, is Negative Tech Tieback.

We need rigorous analog fallbacks and ways to verify whether a technology possesses a positive, measurable tieback to physical reality. Without it, we are left with digital vaporware valued only by speculative theorists, while pragmatists focus on building systems with real-world utility.

AI is here to stay as a utilitarian tool, much like any other compiler or utility script. However, AGI might be a different story and, if your entire business model is a thin wrapper over someone else's foundation model, you are standing on quicksand. Anything involving physical manipulation of matter — even turning a physical wrench — remains profoundly insulated. While technological change moves simultaneously fast and slow, parabolic and logarithmic, some occupations are safe for eighty years; others may only have eight.

#### Entropy and Digital Reuse

The most robust definition of **entropy** applicable across all closed systems is:

> *The amount of energy in a system that cannot be put into additional use.*

This is why **code reuse** is the bedrock of efficient engineering. When a previous manager writes a reference testimonial for you, they write it once in a `.txt` file. The next time a recruiter asks, they simply copy and paste that exact file into an email. The entropy of that operation is near zero.

Foundation models, by contrast, are high-entropy generators. Because prompt spaces and token generation paths are statistically variable, every invocation burns fresh compute to re-derive answers that are probabilistically unique, rather than reliably reusable.
